# Psychoanalytic & Psychodynamic Theoretical Traditions

Use this reference to draw upon specific traditions whenever applicable to the user's inquiry, grounding interpretations in established concepts rather than vague psycho-babble.

## 1. Classical Freudian (Drive & Conflict Model)
- **Core Concepts:** Id, Ego, Superego; pleasure principle vs. reality principle; primary vs. secondary process thinking; return of the repressed; compromise formation.
- **Defenses:** Repression, reaction formation, displacement, condensation, isolation of affect, projection, sublimation.
- **Application Focus:** Internal conflict between desire and prohibition; guilt and self-punitive mechanisms; slips of the tongue (*parapraxes*); manifest vs. latent dream content.

## 2. Object Relations (Klein, Fairbairn, Bion)
- **Core Concepts:** Internalized object relations; part-objects vs. whole-objects; splitting; projective identification; the paranoid-schizoid position (fragmentation, black-and-white splitting, persecutory anxiety) vs. the depressive position (integration, ambivalence, reparation, concern).
- **Application Focus:** How early relational blueprints dictate current interpersonal dynamics; polarization into all-good and all-bad camps; anxieties around annihilation vs. loss.

## 3. Relational, Developmental & Self Psychology (Winnicott, Kohut)
- **Winnicott:**
  - *Holding Environment:* A sufficiently supportive context that allows safe psychological unfolding.
  - *Transitional Object / Transitional Space:* The creative intermediate area between internal reality and external world (play, art, cultural life, tools).
  - *True Self vs. False Self:* Adaptation to environment at the cost of authentic feeling; "good-enough" caretaking.
- **Kohut (Self Psychology):**
  - *Self-objects:* Others experienced as maintaining the cohesion and vitality of the self.
  - *Mirroring, Idealizing, and Twinship needs:* Narcissistic vulnerability, injury, rage, and compensatory defenses.

## 4. Lacanian Structuralism
- **Core Registers:**
  - *The Imaginary:* The realm of ego-identifications, mirror images, illusions of wholeness, and rivalry.
  - *The Symbolic:* The realm of language, law, social contracts, and the Big Other (*Grand Autre*).
  - *The Real:* That which resists symbolization, the traumatic core that escapes language.
- **Key Concepts:** The Signifier; desire of the Other; *objet petit a* (the unattainable object-cause of desire); *point de capiton* (anchoring point of meaning); the structural *lack*.
- **Application Focus:** Close attention to linguistic signifiers, gaps in discourse, homophonic puns, and structural contradictions rather than biological drives.

## 5. Analytical Psychology (Jung)
- **Core Concepts:** Personal vs. collective unconscious; archetypes (Shadow, Anima/Animus, Persona, Self, Trickster); individuation (integration of opposites into a unified self); active imagination; synchronicity.
- **Amplification Method:** Expanding personal imagery through mythological, historical, and cultural parallels rather than reducing symbols to repressed drives.

## 6. Systems Psychodynamics (Tavistock Tradition: Bion, Menzies Lyth, Hirschhorn)
- **Core Concepts:** Group psychology and organizational anxiety; social systems as defenses against anxiety; boundaries, roles, and authorization.
- **Bion's Basic Assumptions:** Groups in distress regress to *Fight-Flight* (seeking enemies), *Dependency* (clinging to a single leader), or *Pairing* (hoping for a future savior/solution).
